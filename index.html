My coding story

Two years ago my big sister showed me a website called Scratch. I snapped a few blocks together, a cat walked across the screen, and I thought it was the coolest thing I had ever made. I have been building games ever since.

Pretty much everything after that I have taught myself. I watch YouTube tutorials, pause them halfway, try the blocks out and see what happens. My family helps when I am properly stuck, but most of it I work out on my own.

I draw my own sprites, put the code together, pick the sounds, and then I test the game about a hundred times until it actually feels fun. Usually my sister plays it first and tells me what is broken.

When one turns out really well I put it online so anyone in the world can play it. I film my own tutorials too now. Those videos taught me everything I know, so it feels pretty good to be the one making them for the next kid who is just starting out.

And I am going after something big. I want to beat the world record for the youngest video game developer. In November I made my attempt at UBC, and right now we are getting all the proof together to send off.

I made my attempt

JUST HAPPENED

In November 2026 I went for the Guinness World Record for Youngest Videogame Developer (Male). I did it at UBC in Vancouver, hosted by the UBC Game Dev club. The rule is that you build a whole game on your own with people watching, and nobody is allowed to help you, not even a little bit.

WHERE IT HAPPENED
UBC, Vancouver
Hosted by the UBC Game Dev club. Sitting in a real university building to do it made the whole thing feel very serious.
WHAT HAPPENS NEXT
Sending in the proof
We are putting all the evidence together for Guinness now. After that comes the really hard part, which is waiting to hear back.
